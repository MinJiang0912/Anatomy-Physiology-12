Transcription

A segment of DNA is copied into RNA. All three classes of RNA are made this way, but here we focus on mRNA.

  1. The DNA unwinds and unzips

    A segment of the double helix opens up. Only one strand, the template strand, gets copied. The other is the inactive strand.

  2. RNA nucleotides pair with the template

    Free RNA nucleotides from the nucleotide pool in the nucleus pair with the exposed DNA bases: A with U, T with A, G with C, and C with G.

  3. RNA polymerase joins them

    The enzyme RNA polymerase links the RNA nucleotides together, producing an RNA molecule.

  4. mRNA carries codons

    The finished mRNA has a sequence of codons that are complementary to the DNA triplet code. The code has been transcribed, or copied.

Key idea. After transcription, mRNA has a base sequence complementary to one of the DNA strands, so its codons are complementary to the DNA triplets.

Template strand

The DNA strand that is read and copied into mRNA.

Inactive strand

The partner DNA strand that is not copied.

Triplet vs codon

A triplet is three DNA bases. A codon is the matching three mRNA bases.

Processing of mRNA

Most human genes are interrupted by segments that don't code for protein. Before mRNA leaves the nucleus, those segments are cut out.

Introns

Intragene segments. They are transcribed but removed, so they are not expressed.

Exons

The parts that are ultimately expressed. Only exons result in a protein product.

Primary vs mature mRNA

Primary mRNAMature mRNA
ContainsIntrons and exonsOnly exons, joined together
LocationNucleus, right after transcriptionLeaves the nucleus for the cytoplasm

Who does the splicing? Ribozymes, which are organic catalysts made of RNA, not protein. Processing happens in the nucleus.

Alternative splicing

Usually all exons are joined. Sometimes, especially during development, only some exons are kept. This produces different mRNA molecules and different but related proteins.
