The wall of the alveolus and the capillary are thin (simple squamous epithelium), so gases diffuse across from high to low concentration.

10 mix-ups to watch for

  1. Glottis vs epiglottis: the glottis is the opening; the epiglottis is the flap that covers it.
  2. Pharynx vs larynx: the pharynx is the throat, shared by air and food; the larynx is the voice box, for air only.
  3. Trachea vs esophagus: the trachea carries air and sits in front; the esophagus carries food and sits behind.
  4. External vs internal respiration: external is lungs (air ↔ blood); internal is tissues (blood ↔ tissue fluid).
  5. Respiration vs breathing: breathing is only 1 of the 4 parts.
  6. Pitch vs loudness: pitch depends on tension; loudness on amplitude.
  7. Pulmonary arteriole vs venule: the arteriole carries O₂-poor blood (blue); the venule carries O₂-rich blood (red).
  8. Direction of the cilia: upward, toward the pharynx, not down into the lungs.
  9. C-rings: the open side faces the esophagus, at the back.
  10. Smell: interpreted in the brain; the nose only has the receptors.
