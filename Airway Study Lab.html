Gas exchange at an alveolus

The wall of the alveolus and the capillary are thin (simple squamous epithelium), so gases diffuse across from high to low concentration.

This is external respiration. The capillary turns from blue to red because the blood picks up O₂ as it passes.

Why the tracheal rings are C-shaped

A slice across the neck. The cartilage holds the trachea open, and the gap faces the esophagus behind it.

At rest: the ring keeps the airway open so it can't collapse.

The 4 parts of respiration

PartWhat happensWhere
1. BreathingAir moves into and out of the lungsAirways, lungs
2. External respirationGas exchange between air and bloodAlveoli and capillaries
3. Internal respirationGas exchange between blood and tissue fluidCapillaries in body tissues
4. Cellular respirationCells make ATP, using O₂ and making CO₂Inside cells

Blood at the alveoli

VesselCarriesDirection
Pulmonary arteriole (blue)O₂-poor bloodAway from the heart, to the alveoli
Pulmonary venule (red)O₂-rich bloodFrom the alveoli toward the heart

Vessels are named by direction: arteries go away from the heart, veins go toward it.
